# AI Vehicle Damage Triage

## 1. Project Overview

AI Vehicle Damage Triage is a computer vision project that analyzes a photo of visible exterior vehicle damage and provides a first-pass assessment.

The system is designed to help drivers and repair professionals quickly understand:

- The likely type of visible damage
- The model's confidence
- How urgent an inspection may be
- The recommended next action
- An indicative repair-cost category

This system is a decision-support tool. It does not replace a qualified mechanic, vehicle inspector, insurance assessor, or repair quotation.

---

## 2. Problem

Drivers often struggle to understand whether visible vehicle damage is:

- Purely cosmetic
- Safe to leave temporarily
- In need of professional inspection
- Potentially urgent

Repair shops also spend time performing basic initial assessments before deciding what kind of inspection or repair is required.

This project improves the first-pass visual triage process by using artificial intelligence to analyze a vehicle image and return a clear, structured preliminary assessment.

---

## 3. Target Users

### Primary users

- **Car drivers** who want an initial indication of visible exterior damage
- **Mechanics and repair shops** that want preliminary information before a full inspection

### Possible future users

- Car rental companies
- Insurance claims teams
- Used-car marketplaces
- Fleet-management companies
- Vehicle inspection services

---

## 4. Input

The first version accepts one image containing one main vehicle and one clearly visible exterior damage area.

### Supported formats

- JPG
- JPEG
- PNG

### Recommended image conditions

For the best result, the image should:

- Clearly show the damaged area
- Contain one main vehicle
- Be taken in good lighting
- Be reasonably close to the damaged part
- Avoid excessive blur
- Avoid strong reflections
- Avoid large obstructions
- Show visible exterior damage

The first version is not designed to diagnose internal mechanical problems from an exterior image.

---

## 5. Output

The system returns:

1. **Predicted damage type**
2. **Confidence score**
3. **Grad-CAM visualization**
4. **Initial urgency level**
5. **Recommended next action**
6. **Indicative cost category**
7. **Human-review warning when necessary**

### Example output

```text
Detected damage: Dent
Confidence: 87.4%
Urgency: Inspection recommended
Cost category: Medium
Recommended action: Request a professional bodywork assessment
```

The cost category is only an approximate indication. It is not a repair quotation.

---

## 6. Machine-Learning Task

Version 1 uses **image classification**.

The model receives one vehicle image and predicts the main visible damage category.

### Why classification is appropriate for Version 1

Classification is suitable because:

- It matches the current project scope
- It can be built using transfer learning
- It is easier to train and evaluate than detection or segmentation
- It is appropriate for learning core computer vision concepts
- It creates a strong foundation for future versions

### Future machine-learning extensions

Future versions may use:

- **Object detection** to locate one or more damaged areas
- **Segmentation** to identify the exact damaged pixels
- **Multi-label classification** when several damage types appear in one image
- **Regression** to estimate repair cost using real repair invoices
- **Multi-image learning** to analyze several vehicle angles
- **Video analysis** for vehicle walkaround inspections

---

## 7. Initial Damage Classes

The model classes must represent visible exterior damage types only.

Suggested initial classes:

1. `no_visible_damage`
2. `scratch`
3. `dent`
4. `bumper_damage`
5. `broken_or_cracked_light`
6. `cracked_glass`

The final classes may change depending on the selected dataset.

### Categories that should not be model classes

The following are not damage types and should not be used as classification labels:

- Urgent fix
- Can wait
- Superficial fix
- Costly
- Cheap
- Normal

These should be generated by a separate triage layer after the damage type is predicted.

### Categories excluded from Version 1

The first version should not attempt to classify:

- Engine problems
- Brake problems
- Steering problems
- Suspension problems
- Airbag problems
- Internal mechanical failures
- Wheel-alignment problems

These problems cannot be diagnosed reliably from a single exterior image.

## 8. System Pipeline

```text
Vehicle image
    ↓
Image preprocessing
    ↓
Damage classification model
    ↓
Confidence check
    ↓
Grad-CAM explanation
    ↓
Rule-based triage layer
    ↓
Structured assessment report
```

The classification model predicts the visible damage type.

The triage layer converts that prediction into:

- Urgency
- Cost category
- Recommended action
- Human-review decision

---

## 9. Triage Logic

The urgency recommendation is generated after classification.

| Predicted damage | Initial urgency | Indicative cost category | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|
| No visible damage | Low priority | None | Continue normal use while monitoring the vehicle |
| Scratch | Low priority | Low | Cosmetic repair when convenient |
| Dent | Inspection recommended | Medium | Request a bodywork assessment |
| Bumper damage | Inspection recommended | Medium or high | Inspect the bumper structure and nearby sensors |
| Broken or cracked light | Urgent inspection | Medium or high | Arrange professional inspection and repair |
| Cracked glass | Urgent inspection | Medium or high | Request professional inspection before continued use |

These rules are preliminary and must be reviewed with automotive professionals before any real-world deployment.

---

## 10. Human Review Rules

The system must refuse to provide a confident automated recommendation when:

- Model confidence is below the selected threshold
- The image is blurry
- The image is too dark
- The damaged area is not visible
- Several damage types appear in the same image
- The vehicle part is not represented in the training dataset
- The image contains unusual or severe damage
- The model focuses on irrelevant background areas
- The input is not a vehicle image
- The damage may affect vehicle safety

The system should also require professional inspection when the user reports:

- Braking problems
- Steering problems
- Unusual sounds
- Smoke
- Fluid leakage
- Warning lights
- Tyre damage
- Wheel damage
- Suspension problems
- Airbag deployment
- Engine problems

### Human-review message

> This image cannot be assessed reliably. A professional vehicle inspection is recommended. Do not continue driving if the vehicle appears unsafe.

## 11. Success Metrics

### 11.1 Machine-learning metrics

The project should evaluate:

- **Accuracy**
- **Macro F1-score**
- **Per-class precision**
- **Per-class recall**
- **Confusion matrix**
- **Validation loss**
- **Test loss**
- **Low-confidence prediction rate**
- **Human-review referral rate**
- **Inference time**

### 11.2 Important safety-oriented metrics

Particular attention should be given to:

- Recall for cracked glass
- Recall for broken or cracked lights
- False negatives for serious visible damage
- Incorrect predictions with high confidence
- Cases where Grad-CAM focuses on irrelevant regions

### 11.3 Product metrics

The product should also measure:

- Average assessment time
- Percentage of valid uploads
- Percentage of cases referred for human review
- Agreement with mechanic assessments
- User understanding of the report
- Reduction in initial assessment time

### 11.4 Initial project targets

Initial prototype targets:

- Macro F1-score of at least `0.75`
- Recall of at least `0.85` for urgent visible-damage classes
- Inference time below `3 seconds`
- Automatic human review for low-confidence cases
- No exact repair-cost claims
- Clear limitations displayed in the application

These are development targets and not guaranteed results.

---

## 12. Limitations

Version 1 will not:

- Diagnose internal mechanical problems
- Determine whether a vehicle is legally safe to drive
- Replace a professional mechanic
- Replace an insurance assessor
- Provide an official vehicle inspection
- Produce an exact repair quotation
- Make insurance claim decisions
- Evaluate hidden structural damage
- Evaluate damage under the vehicle
- Reliably analyze several damaged areas in one image
- Detect all possible vehicle-damage types
- Consider the vehicle model, year, mileage, or maintenance history
- Consider regional labour prices
- Consider exact replacement-part prices
- Work reliably on poor-quality images
- Guarantee correct predictions outside the training-data distribution

---

## 13. Ethical and Safety Considerations

The application must:

- Clearly state that results are preliminary
- Avoid telling users that a vehicle is definitely safe
- Redirect uncertain cases to professional inspection
- Display model confidence
- Display model limitations
- Avoid presenting estimated cost as a factual quotation
- Avoid making legal or insurance decisions
- Protect uploaded user images
- Avoid storing images without user consent
- Document training-data limitations and possible bias

---

## 14. Version 1 Goal

The goal of Version 1 is to build and deploy a reliable prototype that can:

- Classify common visible exterior vehicle damage
- Display prediction confidence
- Explain predictions with Grad-CAM
- Generate a cautious first-pass urgency recommendation
- Redirect uncertain or safety-related cases to human review
- Present results through a simple web application
